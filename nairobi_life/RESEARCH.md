## 1. CLIMATE

### 1.1 Altitude, pressure, location
- **Altitude:** Nairobi sits at ~1,795 m (some say 1,661–1,863 m depending on district; Dagoretti in the west is ~1,850 m). Sources: https://www.nearweather.com/location/nairobi-ke-184745 , https://www.weatherandclimate.com/kenya/nairobi-city
- **Typical air pressure:** ~1,015 hPa / ~30.01 inHg (sea-level-adjusted station pressure at altitude is lower, ~835–850 hPa; the widely cited 1,015 hPa is the standardised value). Source: https://www.timeanddate.com/weather/kenya/nairobi/climate
- **Latitude:** 1.29° S (almost exactly on the equator). Source: https://www.weatherandclimate.com/kenya/nairobi-city

### 1.4 Seasons (the part a game MUST get right)
Nairobi has **two rainy seasons and two dry seasons**, NOT a European four-season pattern:

- **Long rains ("masika"):** roughly **March–May**, peaking April. This is the wettest period. Source: https://www.climatestotravel.com/climate/kenya/nairobi , https://www.nearweather.com/location/nairobi-ke-184745
- **Short rains ("mvua ya vuli"):** roughly **October–December**, peaking November. Source: same.
- **Long dry / cool season:** **June–September** — coolest, driest, and **least sunny** (overcast "winter"). Source: https://www.climatestotravel.com/climate/kenya/nairobi , https://plantrip.io/weather-expert/209 ("cool cloudy season June–August… persistent grey overcast skies… fine mist known locally as *gathandara*")
- **Hot dry season:** **January–February**. Source: same.

> ⚠️ **Common wrong assumption:** that "winter" in Nairobi is hot/dry (like a tropical cliché) or that it rains in summer. Nairobi's "winter" (Jun–Aug) is the *cool, grey, driest* time. The heavy rains come in spring (Mar–May) and late autumn (Oct–Dec).

### 1.5 Time of day rain falls
East African convection peaks in the **afternoon and early evening** — rain typically comes as **heavy afternoon/evening downpours** (often 30 min–1 hr bursts), not all-day drizzle. Source (general E. Africa diurnal convective peak, afternoon/early evening): https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2019JD031167 ("enhancement is more prominent during the wettest part of the day, generally the afternoon"). Travel sources describe Nairobi rains similarly: "rain falls as a torrential downpour lasting perhaps half an hour to an hour, with the sun then coming out." Source: https://www.expertafrica.com/kenya/weather-and-climate
- **Long rains** tend to fall as sustained afternoon showers; **short rains** are shorter, more scattered bursts.

### 1.6 Humidity
- Annual average ~**70–73%**; range across months ~53% (Feb, driest) to ~76% (May, wettest). Source: https://www.timeanddate.com/weather/kenya/nairobi/climate , https://en.climate-data.org/africa/kenya/nairobi/nairobi-541/
- Mornings are humid; afternoons drier.

|---|---|---|---|
| Around Jun 21 (solstice) | ~06:33 | ~18:35 | ~11 h 58 m |

**Day length varies only ~12–14 minutes across the whole year.** There is essentially no "long summer evening." Sunset is ~18:35–18:40 year-round. For the game: a fixed clock of ~06:30 sunrise / ~18:40 sunset is realistic every day.

### 1.8 Wind
- Typical speeds **~9–15 kph** (light breeze). Windiest around March (17 kph); calmest around May–Aug (~9–10 kph). Source: https://www.climatestotravel.com/climate/kenya/nairobi
- Prevailing direction: **south-westerly / south-easterly trade winds**; the **kusi** (SE monsoon, cool/moist) blows roughly Apr–Oct, the **kaskazi** (NE monsoon, dry) Nov–Mar. Source: https://www.expertafrica.com/kenya/weather-and-climate
- ⚠️ Not a "hot windy savanna" — Nairobi wind is gentle and cool.

### 1.9 Air quality / PM2.5
- **IQAir, Aug 2026:** PM2.5 ~**15.5 µg/m³** (US AQI ~63, "Moderate"), about 3.1× the WHO annual guideline. Source: https://www.iqair.com/air-quality/kenya/nairobi/nairobi
- **aqi.in** reported PM2.5 ~12 µg/m³ ("Good") in late 2025. Source: https://www.aqi.in/dashboard/kenya/nairobi-city/nairobi
- 2020 annual average was 14.7 µg/m³ (dirtiest month July ~22.6). Source: https://www.iqair.com/air-quality/kenya/nairobi/nairobi
- **Major local sources:** vehicle exhaust (matatus, boda bodas, old diesels), road dust, open burning, industry. Vehicle density + altitude basin traps pollution.
- Nairobi is **not** a clean-air city; "Moderate" haze is common, worse in dry months and rush hour. Avoid depicting it as pristine mountain air.

### 2.2 Matatu fares (Nairobi, 2026 — IMPORTANT: fares spiked in 2026)
Two fuel-driven hikes hit Nairobi in 2026:
- **15 Apr 2026:** Matatu Owners Association announced **+25%** after EPRA raised diesel. Source: https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/122613-nairobi-commuters-pay-ksh50-more-matatu-fares-jump-25-high-fuel-prices
- **15 May 2026:** operators announced **+50%** and a nationwide strike. Source: https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/123441-matatu-operators-nairobi-announce-50-fare-hike-nationwide-strike-monday
- Fuel has since **stabilised**: Super Petrol **KSh 214.03/l**, Diesel **KSh 222.86/l** (Jul 15–Aug 14 2026 cycle). Source: https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/125252-epra-retains-fuel-prices-petrol-diesel-and-kerosene-costs-remain-unchanged-until-august

**Current (post-hike, mid-2026) representative CBD fares** (from the 25% hike notice, which is the detailed one; 50% hike was a peak/protest spike, many routes later settled nearer the 25% level):

|---|---|---|
| Kawangware / Kibera / Kangemi / Mathare | ~80 → ~100 | ~100 |
| Eastleigh / Ngara / Pangani / Parklands (short) | ~30 → ~40 | ~40–60 |
| South B / South C / Industrial Area / Westlands (short) | ~50 → ~60–65 | ~65 |
| Umoja / Donholm / Embakasi / Pipeline (off-peak) | ~70–100 | ~125–130 |
| Thika Rd (Roysambu, Kasarani, Githurai) | ~100 | ~125–200 |
| Rongai / Ngong / Ruaka / Kikuyu / Thika (long) | ~150 | ~190 |

Source: https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/122613-nairobi-commuters-pay-ksh50-more-matatu-fares-jump-25-high-fuel-prices , corroborated by 2025 route list https://derrickingarah.wixsite.com/jiupdate/post/matatu-fare-in-nairobi-what-commuters-pay-on-different-routes-in-2025
**Rules of thumb for the game:** fares rise in **rain**, at **rush hour (06:30–09:00, 17:00–19:00)**, and on weekends/holidays when matatus are scarce. Light rain can push a fare up ~50%; a downpour can *double* it ("if it rains, fares can double" — https://derrickingarah.wixsite.com/jiupdate). Conductors (makanga) tell you the fare; round numbers, cash only, little change given.

### 2.3 Boda boda (motorcycle taxi) fare
- **Short trip: KSh 100–300** typical; a representative Nairobi ride ~KSh 200. Source: https://safarikenyaexplorer.com/what-things-cost-in-kenya/ , https://urbannext.net/boda-boda/ ("a typical ride costs about 200 KSh")
- Riders can earn up to ~KSh 3,000/day (variable). Source: https://urbannext.net/boda-boda/ , https://grokipedia.com/page/Boda_boda

### 2.4 Kibera / informal-settlement housing
⚠️ **DO NOT use the "houseforrentkenya.com" Kibera listings (KSh 10,000–80,000/mo)** — those are SEO-template figures for the *formal* fringe, not slum shacks. Real informal single-room rents are far lower.
- Reliable figures: Mathare study (2020, representative of Nairobi informal settlements) found **single-room shack rents KSh 1,800–3,000/month**, average ~KSh 2,000–3,000; tenements ~double shack rates. Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0956247820942166 (Table 4)
- Older Kibera-specific sources cite **~KSh 700–2,000/month** for a single room. Source: https://thedarkatlas.com/posts/kibera-nairobi-slum ("single room costs around 700 KSh per month") — note this may be a low outlier/older figure; the 1,800–3,000 range is safer for a 2025/26 setting.
- Structure owners (landlords) are mostly **absentee** (govt officials, politicians, Kikuyu/Nubian) — tenants (often Luo, Luhya, Kamba) have no tenure. Source: https://thedarkatlas.com/posts/kibera-nairobi-slum

**Use in game:** a Kibera single room ≈ **KSh 2,000–3,000/month** (not thousands). This is a rental market, not free squatting.

### 2.9 Clinic visit & malaria treatment
- **Public clinic consultation:** government dispensaries are often **free or ~KSh 10–100** for locals; private clinic consult **KSh 500–2,000+**. Source: https://nairobionline.com/articles/how-much-does-it-cost-to-see-a-consultant-doctor-in-kenya.html (consultant KSh 500–17,000), https://safarikenyaexplorer.com/healthcare-costs-in-kenya/
- **Malaria test (RDT):** KSh 200–500. **ACT treatment (Artemether-Lumefantrine, first-line):** a course commonly **KSh 200–600** at pharmacy/dispensary. Source: https://kenyaembassyinfo.com/malaria-prevention
- ⚠️ **MAJOR CORRECTION — Malaria in Nairobi:** Nairobi is **NOT a malaria zone**. At ~1,795 m the city is classified **very low / negligible risk**; most travel-medicine sources say antimalarials are not needed for a Nairobi-only stay. Sources: https://travelsafesos.com/kenya/malaria-risk-kenya-travellers/ , https://kenyaembassyinfo.com/malaria-prevention ("Nairobi: Very Low… altitude 1,795m; occasional cases but very rare"). Burden is in western Kenya (Lake Victoria) and the coast.
  - **For the game:** depicting Nairobi characters constantly fearing or dying of malaria is inaccurate. Mosquitoes exist, but malaria is rare; "malaria treatment" is a thing people buy for upcountry travel, not a daily Nairobi reality. Don't make malaria a Nairobi street hazard.

### 3.2 County askaris vs hawkers in the CBD (2025 rules)
- **As of 6 Jan 2025**, Nairobi County (Gov. Sakaja) **banned hawking on main CBD streets** (Moi Ave, Haile Selassie, Latema, River Rd, Ronald Ngala, etc.). Hawking allowed **only on designated backstreets/lanes, Mon–Sat, 4 pm–10 pm**. Source: https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/realtime/2025-01-09-hawkers-will-not-be-allowed-in-nairobi-cbd-sakaja , https://jedcamedia.com/nairobi-county-bans-hawking-in-main-streets-in-cbd
- By **July 2025** the county was threatening to **arrest customers** buying from illegal hawkers, not just vendors. Source: https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/114525-nairobi-county-arrest-buyers-and-hawkers-operating-illegal-areas
- **Dynamic:** askaris (kanjo) **confiscate goods**, impound boda bodas, and historically **extort bribes** (KSh 2,000–3,000 "fine" or bribe; bike storage fees accrue daily). Source: https://nation.africa/kenya/counties/nairobi/how-nairobi-cbd-became-one-big-boda-boda-bazaar-3620524 , https://www.kbc.co.ke/news/kidero-suspends-county-askaris-over-extorting-hawkers/ (2016 extortion case)
- Public sympathy usually sides with hawkers; crowds sometimes force askaris to return goods. Source: https://www.tiktok.com/@ntvkenya/video/7644565954395131143
- **For the game:** hawking is semi-legal, a cat-and-mouse with kanjo; confiscation + bribe is the core tension, not "police arrest for loitering."

### 3.3 Daily rhythm of the city
- **Rush hours:** ~**06:30–09:00** and **17:00–19:00** (matatu fares peak, roads gridlocked). Source: https://derrickingarah.wixsite.com/jiupdate/post/matatu-fare-in-nairobi-what-commuters-pay-on-different-routes-in-2025
- **Markets busy:** early morning (mama mboga, Gikomba) through midday; backstreet hawking surges after **4 pm**.
- **Midday:** CBD hectic, matatus full.
- **Evening:** streets empty relatively early — **sun sets ~18:40, most informal trade winds down by 21:00–22:00**; few walk around late except entertainment zones (Westlands, clubs) and night-shift workers.
- **Sunday:** much quieter; many businesses closed, churches full.

### 3.4 What heavy rain does to Kibera & informal settlements
- Nairobi sits on the **Nairobi River floodplain**; informal settlements (Kibera, Mathare, Mukuru) line **river valleys and low-lying land**. Source: https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-africa-68898731
- Heavy/long rains → **flash floods, knee-to-waist-deep water, mud, collapsed paths**, **open drains/sewers overflow** (less than half the city is on sewer; slums rely on open channels). Source: https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-africa-68898731 , https://www.wri.org/research/flooding-nairobis-informal-settlements
- **2024 floods:** ~294 deaths nationally, Nairobi county hardest hit; Mathare/Kibera/Mukuru worst; homes flooded, roofs sheltered people, "flying toilets" returned after toilet/sewer lines destroyed. Source: https://www.preventionweb.net/news/life-after-kenyas-floods-2024
- **March 2026 flash floods:** Nairobi again hit; Mathare, Kibra, South C, Mukuru submerged. Source: https://www.rivermixer.com/nairobi-kenya-flood-update-nairobi-mathare-river-levels-rising-in-mathare-south-c-and-kibra/ , https://jbagr.com/insights/flood-risk-in-nairobi-lessons-from-the-march-2026-flash-floods/
- **For the game:** in rainy season, Kibera paths become rivers of mud; bridges/planks over streams are the only crossings; a downpour can strand residents for hours. This is a real, recurring mechanic — not a one-off disaster.

### 3.6 Real Nairobi places worth referencing
- **CBD** (Central Business District) — offices, matatu termini (Accra Rd, Moi Ave, Ronald Ngala, Kencom).
- **Kibera** — largest informal settlement, SW of CBD (villages: Gatwekera, Kisumu Ndogo, Lindi, Soweto, Makina).
- **Industrial Area** — southeast; factories, loaders, jua kali workshops.
- **Ngong Road** — major artery SW (UON, Adams Arcade, Kawangware end).
- **Mombasa Road** — highway SE to airport/JKIA, Embakasi, Pipeline.
- **Gikomba market** — huge open-air market (Mitumba/clothes, produce); fires common.
- **Eastleigh** — Somali-owned commercial hub; textiles, phones; also residential.
- **Kawangware** — dense low-income estate, Ngong Rd.

## 4. AUTHENTICITY & RESPECT GUIDANCE

### 4.1 Avoid harmful stereotypes / caricature
- ❌ **"Nairobi = slum + safari."** Show the full city: high-rises, tech hubs, universities, malls, middle-class estates — not only Kibera.
- ❌ **Poverty-porn / helplessness.** Residents are resourceful operators in a harsh system (rent-paying tenants, hustlers, organised SACCOs). Show agency, not victimhood.
- ❌ **All hawkers = criminals.** Hawking is survival under restrictive by-laws; the conflict is structural (kanjo corruption, lack of space), not personal villainy.
- ❌ **Everyone speaks "tribal gibberish" or exaggerated Sheng.** Use Sheng sparingly and correctly; most Nairobians mix English/Swahili/Sheng by context.
- ❌ **Malaria everywhere / wild animals in streets.** Nairobi is highland, urban, low-malaria. No lions in the CBD (the Nairobi National Park is fenced and separate).
- ❌ **"African time" / lazy tropes.** Punctuality and hustle are real; informal work is hard, not leisurely.
- ❌ **Uniform ethnicity.** Nairobi is multi-ethnic (Kikuyu, Luo, Luhya, Kamba, Somali, Kalenjin, etc.); don't make it mono-ethnic.

### 4.2 What makes it dignified and true
- Show **interdependence**: mama mboga feeds the estate, boda connects it, askari enforces messy rules, structure owners profit, NGOs fill gaps.
- Show **cost-of-living maths**: a KSh 2,000 room, KSh 150 plate, KSh 100 boda, KSh 50 toilet — the daily calculus is the story.
- Show **climate as character**: cool grey July, April downpours flooding Kibera, the 18:40 sunset every day.
- Show **informal economy as skilled labour**: mjengo, mkokoteni, jua kali welders are craftspeople.
- Use **correct, current prices** (this doc) rather than vague "cheap Africa" gestures.
- Depict **real places** with their actual role (Gikomba = market, Eastleigh = commerce, Industrial Area = labour).
